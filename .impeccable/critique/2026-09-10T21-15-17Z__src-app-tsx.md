## Overall Impression

The game already has the hard part: a memorable, product-specific interface whose phases, danger states, and tactical vocabulary reinforce one another. The largest opportunity is to make that command deck genuinely operational: readable at the compact viewport it supports, truthful in reduced-motion mode, and usable without a precision pointer.

## What's Working

- **Corruption is a system state, not a skin.** The global accent, vignette, HUD language, card behavior, and end-state treatment all participate in the reversal. That directly serves the game's defining mechanic.
- **The turn loop has strong procedural hierarchy.** `BEGIN SEQUENCE`, draw, select, play/discard, targeting, conflict preparation, and result states are staged instead of presented simultaneously. The activity log and specific disabled reasons preserve cause and effect.
- **Learning and replay are unusually well supported.** The guided tutorial, in-game Field Manual, persistent setup preferences, match record, and replay-seed action give both new and returning players useful paths without leaving the game.

## Priority Issues

### 1. [P1] Core play is pointer-only inside an inaccessible canvas

**Why it matters:** Phaser cards and player targets use `setInteractive()` and `pointerdown`, while the source contains no gameplay keyboard navigation. The DOM shell does not provide an equivalent hand, target list, or live state model. A keyboard-only or screen-reader player cannot complete the primary loop. Setup adds unlabeled text/range inputs, segmented buttons without `aria-pressed`, and glyph-only controls whose visible characters are not meaningful names. `OverlayShell` declares dialogs, but it does not move, trap, or return focus; Help, About, Options, and Pause do not consistently use it.

**Fix:** Add a DOM-accessible control layer for hand selection and player targeting, with roving keyboard focus, visible `:focus-visible` treatment, semantic control states, an `aria-live` turn/result channel, and focus capture/return for every modal. Keep the Phaser canvas visual, but do not make it the only interaction surface.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### 2. [P1] Reduce Animations does not fulfill its own promise

**Why it matters:** Options says the setting disables card tweens, scanlines, and panel wipes, but source gating is partial. `App.tsx` keeps two infinite game scanlines; HUD pulses and flicker continue; GlitchTitle, Help, About, Options, Pause, and Game Over retain CSS animation. There is no `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback. Users who deliberately opt out still receive continuous motion and flashing emphasis.

**Fix:** Drive every React and Phaser motion path from one motion policy, mirror it with `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)`, remove continuous scan/flicker/pulse effects when reduced, and make modal open/close immediate without delayed animation timers. Preserve state with static borders, copy, and color-independent symbols.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable animate`

### 3. [P1] Compact landscape preserves geometry by sacrificing legibility

**Why it matters:** The responsive layout test proves that the board bands do not overlap, but its 667x375 policy scales the hand to 0.5. Card typography begins at 7-11px, so the source calculation predicts effective text around 3.5-5.5px. Player-zone and centre telemetry also starts at 5-10px, and compact HUD text drops to 7-10px. The tiny sort controls explicitly set `minHeight: 0`. Actions may remain on-screen while the tactical information needed to choose them becomes unreadable or hard to tap.

**Fix:** Change topology instead of scaling the whole instrument: keep the hand compact but expose the selected card in a fixed readable inspector, collapse secondary telemetry, retain at least 12-14px operational text and 44px targets, and validate 667x375, 896x414, and 1024x576 with rendered screenshots and actual taps.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### 4. [P1] The advertised five-player mode is unreachable from setup

**Why it matters:** README and PRODUCT.md promise one human plus one to four AI opponents. State restoration accepts `crg-count` values through 4, and `startGame(count + 1)` supports that model, but Setup renders only `[1, 2, 3]`. A normal user cannot choose four AI opponents, and `NUMBER OF AGENTS` is ambiguous about whether the human is included.

**Fix:** Add a fourth opponent choice wherever the layout supports it and label the control `AI OPPONENTS`; if five-player play is no longer supported, reduce the state range and product promise together. Make portrait constraints explicit without silently mutating a prior selection.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### 5. [P2] The visual system is coherent but not centralized

**Why it matters:** Repeated near-miss reds, cyans, steel values, alpha suffixes, and dozens of font sizes create subtle inconsistency and make accessibility fixes expensive. Help, About, Options, Pause, and state overlays each rebuild related panel behavior, which is why semantics, safe-area handling, and reduced-motion behavior have drifted.

**Fix:** Create shared semantic tokens usable by CSS and Phaser, including explicit alpha ramps and separate readable canvas/DOM typography ramps. Consolidate modal structure around one accessible shell and retire unused `src/App.css`. Treat the detector clusters as migration groups, not individual tickets.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer):** The boot sequence can occupy about five seconds before revealing setup, although click/key skip is available. `NUMBER OF AGENTS` does not say that the number excludes the human. Once play begins, commands such as `BEGIN SEQUENCE`, `MULTITASKING`, and Corruption-first constraints are on-brand but depend heavily on the tutorial or Field Manual. The four-tab manual helps, yet its dense catalogue is a reference wall rather than contextual guidance at every decision.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent):** The handle and range inputs have no programmatic labels; option segments/toggles do not announce selection state; mobile utility buttons collapse to `?`, music glyphs, circled numerals, and `✦`; focus styling exists only in unused `App.css`; and the primary card/target loop lives in a pointer-driven canvas. Dialog labeling is inconsistent and focus is never trapped or restored. Sam cannot reliably complete a match.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** Portrait mode silently forces opponent count back to one and the compact landscape strategy makes card rules and status telemetry extremely small. Main action buttons are generally placed in useful thumb zones and use 44-48px targets, but hand sorting drops below that standard and top-corner icon controls remain difficult to interpret one-handed.

**Morgan (Solo Tactical Card Player):** Morgan expects to compare six cards, forecast opponent state, and understand every rule resolution. The activity log, target staging, and replay-seed action serve that mindset well, but microtext makes card effects hard to compare on a phone, the fourth AI option is absent, and cryptic compact stats such as `D2`, `5c`, arrows, and unheaded symbols weaken tactical confidence.

## Cognitive Load

**Moderate: 2 of 8 checklist failures.** Phase sequencing, visual grouping, and a dominant primary action manage the game's intrinsic complexity well. The failures are **minimal choices** and **working memory**: a six-card hand necessarily exceeds the four-item comfort threshold, while counter timing, daemon immunities, Corruption inversion, and compact symbol legends require remembered rules. A readable selected-card inspector and context-specific rule reminder would convert some of that memory work into recognition without flattening the strategy.

## Emotional Journey

The opening boot and title establish tension immediately; conflict preparation and the global Corruption switch create clear peaks; and the standings/replay screen gives the session a strong ending. The emotional valley is operational friction: waiting through boot before the first setup decision, shrinking rules until they cannot be read on a supported landscape phone, or asking for reduced motion and continuing to see scans and pulses. High-stakes selections do provide banners, cancel paths, and explicit results, which is reassuring, but that reassurance is visual-only for assistive users.

## Minor Observations

- The boot lasts roughly 5.2 seconds if untouched; because it runs once per page load and is skippable, it is atmospheric polish rather than a blocker, but the skip hint is only 0.5rem and very dim.
- The setup range control has a 14px thumb and 14px total height, substantially smaller than the 44px target standard used elsewhere.
- The Field Manual's four visual tabs lack `role="tablist"`, `role="tab"`, and `aria-selected`; its panel also lacks the shared safe-area-aware `OverlayShell`.
- Pause says `CLICK ANYWHERE TO RESUME`; it does not expose an explicit button or an Escape/Space keyboard path.
- Mobile Game Over hides column details but still uses abbreviations (`c`, `↑`, `[D]`) without persistent column labels.
- The detector's width-transition warning points to the cycle bar. For this small scoreboard it is low impact, but a transform-based fill would animate more cheaply and can be disabled with the same motion policy.
